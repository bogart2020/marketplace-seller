# Pricing & Negotiation

Research conducted 2026-08-27. Primary sources (Meta help center, platform help centers, academic papers, US BEA) prioritized; reseller/SEO blogs marked SECONDARY/UNVERIFIED and used only to fill documented gaps, never as the sole basis for a numeric claim presented as fact.

## 1. Facebook Marketplace official price-related features

**Price-drop notifications to people who saved/watched a listing.**
No official Meta/Facebook Help Center article documents a "notify savers on price drop" feature. I could not locate any help.facebook.com or about.fb.com page that describes this mechanic in writing — repeated fetches of the two candidate Help Center URLs (`facebook.com/help/196733038830912`, `facebook.com/help/561376580709359`) returned only generic listing-creation instructions, with no section on price-change notifications [META-OFFICIAL — checked, not found: https://www.facebook.com/help/561376580709359, https://www.facebook.com/help/196733038830912]. Reseller/SEO blogs uniformly assert that saving an item + a later price cut triggers a notification to the saver, framing it as a known seller tactic [SECONDARY-UNVERIFIED: https://pagecrawl.io/tools/facebook-marketplace-price-drop-alerts.html, https://closo.co/blogs/platform-specific-guides/how-to-edit-price-on-facebook-marketplace]. At least one secondary source directly contradicts this, claiming Facebook does **not** notify buyers of price drops on saved/watched listings [SECONDARY-UNVERIFIED, contradicts the above: search-result summary citing howtogeek.com-style consumer guidance, no single authoritative page found]. **Net finding: this is a widely-repeated but Meta-undocumented claim with a direct secondary-source contradiction. Treat as unconfirmed, not as fact.**

**Price history / "listed X days ago."** No price-history chart or price-change log is documented anywhere in Meta's help pages. Marketplace does show a coarse, rounded relative age ("Listed 3 weeks ago," "listed over a week ago") on listings, per third-party observation — Meta does not document this in an official Help Center article; it is an observed UI behavior only [SECONDARY-UNVERIFIED: search-result summary, no primary Meta doc located]. There is no evidence of a visible price-history graph shown to buyers.

**Listing "staleness" tools.** Meta does document a **Renew** action for listings (available once a listing reaches roughly seven days old, usable a limited number of times) that resets apparent freshness/ranking — this is reported consistently across reseller guides but again I could not locate a Meta Help Center page spelling out the exact rules (age threshold, use-count cap) [SECONDARY-UNVERIFIED: https://socialoapp.com/blog/what-does-renew-listing-mean-on-facebook-marketplace/]. Treat exact thresholds ("7 days," "5 times") as unverified specifics from secondary sources, not confirmed Meta policy.

**AI price suggestions (this IS officially documented).** Meta's own newsroom describes an AI listing tool that "suggest[s] a price based on similar items listed in your area" when a seller uploads photos to auto-draft a listing [META-OFFICIAL: https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/facebook-marketplace-new-meta-ai-tools-make-selling-faster-and-easier/]. The July 2026 "Seller" app (a dedicated app for Marketplace sellers) also bundles a "price suggestion" field into AI-assisted listing creation and adds a "Performance insights" panel (views, clicks, message threads, sold listings) explicitly framed as helping sellers "price competitively" [META-OFFICIAL: https://about.fb.com/news/2026/07/introducing-seller-app-facebook-marketplace/]. This traces back to a similar feature first reported in 2018 (AI-suggested price ranges from photos/description; Meta stated fewer sellers abandoned listings once autosuggest was enabled) [OTHER — press report citing a Facebook company blog post, not directly re-verified as still live in identical form: https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/03/facebook-adds-a-i-to-marketplace-for-categorization-price-suggestions-and-soon-visual-search]. Separately, Meta is testing AI "price insights" specifically for vehicle listings [META-OFFICIAL: https://about.fb.com/news/2025/11/facebook-marketplace-gets-a-glow-up/].

**Verdict:** Meta officially documents an AI price-suggestion tool and a seller performance-insights panel. It does **not** officially document price-drop-to-savers notifications, price history displays, or the exact mechanics of "listed X days ago" — those remain reseller folklore, not confirmed platform behavior.

## 2. Offer / counter-offer mechanic vs. plain chat

Facebook does have a documented "Make an offer" feature, distinct from open chat — the Help Center article exists at a stable URL, but its content could not be retrieved for the US/general version; fetching it from a Philippines-geolocated session returned "This feature is not currently available in your location" with no operational detail [META-OFFICIAL — page exists, content geo-gated: https://www.facebook.com/help/196733038830912]. Secondary reporting describes the mechanic as: buyer taps an "Offer" button, is given quick-pick choices (asking price / ~10% under / custom amount) plus a custom-amount field, and the seller can accept, decline, or counter — after which the negotiation proceeds via Messenger chat regardless [SECONDARY-UNVERIFIED: https://blog.nibbletechnology.com/the-guide-to-make-an-offer-buttons-on-marketplaces/]. **Important, higher-confidence finding: this Offer button appears to be geographically gated and not confirmed live in the Philippines** — the Help Center itself told an agent browsing from a PH-associated session that the feature isn't available in that location [META-OFFICIAL, geo-availability signal: https://www.facebook.com/help/196733038830912]. Where the Offer button is unavailable, all negotiation on Facebook Marketplace is plain Messenger chat with no structured offer/accept/decline state — a buyer just messages a number.

By contrast, **Carousell has a clearly documented, non-geo-gated structured offer mechanic**: a "Make Offer" button (on the listing page or inside chat) that opens a formal proposal the seller can accept, reject, or counter, positioned as a stronger commitment than casual chat — "once the seller has accepted your offer, it is not in the spirit of Carousell to back out of a deal" [OTHER-PLATFORM-PRIMARY: https://support.carousell.com/hc/en-us/articles/115009696128-How-to-deal-safely-on-Carousell, https://blog.carousell.ph/pro-tips/how-buy-on-carousell/]. Carousell also has a separate instant "Buy" button (fixed-price, no negotiation) that some sellers can enable, distinct from Make Offer [OTHER-PLATFORM-PRIMARY: https://support.carousell.com/hc/en-us/articles/17662022748569].

Mercari's negotiation mechanic is "Smart Offers" / seller-initiated and buyer counter-offers within its Smart Pricing ecosystem, but a secondary source reports Mercari **discontinued "Smart Offers"** at some point, with uncertainty about whether that was intentional [SECONDARY-UNVERIFIED: https://www.valueaddedresource.net/mercari-discontinues-smart-offers-changes-promotions/] — I could not verify current status against an official Mercari Help Center page (mercari.com/help_center returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch).

**Verdict:** Carousell > Mercari > Facebook Marketplace in terms of documented, structured offer mechanics. Facebook's is real but may not be live for a Philippines-based user; assume Messenger-only negotiation for PH unless the Offer button is confirmed present in-app.

## 3. "Free" / "Please contact" pricing and $0 listings

Meta's own Help Center instructs sellers that to mark an item as Free, they enter 0 as the price [META-OFFICIAL: https://www.facebook.com/help/561376580709359]. No official Meta documentation of a distinct "Contact for price" listing type was found; third-party guidance (Quora-level, not authoritative) suggests sellers work around the absence of a "POA" field by using the description field or a placeholder price [SECONDARY-UNVERIFIED: https://www.quora.com/How-can-you-put-Contact-Seller-in-place-of-a-set-price-on-Facebook-Marketplace-Any-insights].

On pricing honesty, Meta's Commerce Policies (governing Marketplace, Instagram/WhatsApp commerce) prohibit misleading, fraudulent, or deceptive pricing/offers generally, and secondary compliance guides specifically cite "$1 bait listings to drive messages" and inflated/shill pricing as violations enforceable by listing removal or account restriction [META-OFFICIAL policy existence: https://www.facebook.com/policies_center/commerce; SECONDARY summary of enforcement examples: https://appath.com/resources/common-compliance-violations-on-facebook-marketplace-and-how-to-avoid-them]. I did not find a Meta page stating a hard minimum price floor (e.g., "no listing under $X"); the constraint documented is behavioral/honesty-based, not a numeric floor.

## 4. Comps tooling for real secondhand price research

**eBay sold/completed listings.** eBay's own site supports filtering to Sold Items (checkbox in left-rail filters) and to Sold/Completed via Advanced Search or URL parameters `&LH_Sold=1` / `&LH_Complete=1`; sold data is limited to roughly a 90-day lookback window [OTHER-PLATFORM-PRIMARY, standard eBay search UI behavior, confirmed via community documentation: https://community.ebay.com/t5/Ask-a-Mentor/How-to-find-a-seller-s-completed-and-or-sold-items/td-p/34653601]. **Critical, dated finding: as of July 22, 2026, eBay began redirecting signed-out visitors to a sign-in page on any search carrying the sold or completed filter, across every eBay marketplace** — this is a live-in-2026 access change, not a hypothetical, reported consistently and matching the direct behavior an unauthenticated fetch would hit [SECONDARY reporting citing observed platform behavior, not an eBay press release: search-result synthesis referencing community.ebay.com Seller News threads]. **Practical consequence for an AI agent using only plain web search: eBay sold-comps data is no longer reliably reachable without an eBay login as of mid-2026.** Regular (non-sold, non-completed) active-listing searches remain accessible without login.

**PriceCharting (games/consoles).** Official methodology (per PriceCharting's own FAQ/methodology pages, though direct fetch of those pages returned HTTP 403 to this agent — content characterized via search-result excerpts, not directly re-read): PriceCharting aggregates sold-listing data primarily from eBay plus its own marketplace, and algorithmically computes a current price using recency-weighting, medians/averages, and outlier handling [OTHER-PLATFORM-PRIMARY, via search excerpt of https://www.pricecharting.com/page/methodology — not independently re-verified by direct fetch]. The site is browsable for free without an account (ads shown to logged-out/free users; paid tiers remove ads and add API/bulk-export features) [OTHER-PLATFORM-PRIMARY: search-result excerpt of https://www.pricecharting.com/faq]. Coverage is narrow: video games, consoles, trading cards, comics, LEGO, coins — not general secondhand goods. No PH-specific pricing; prices are USD-denominated and reflect the US/global collector market, so raw PriceCharting values need manual FX/market-discount adjustment for Philippine resale.

**Carousell.** Category/search-result pages on carousell.ph are indexed and directly reachable by plain web search (confirmed empirically: a `site:carousell.ph` search returned live category pages showing current asking prices in PHP) [confirmed via this agent's own WebSearch tool, https://www.carousell.ph/...]. However, Carousell shows **current asking prices on active listings only** — there is no documented "sold/completed" filter equivalent to eBay's, so it is a source of ask-price comps, not verified transaction (sold) comps. This is the single most PH-relevant comps source found in this research given Carousell's stated Philippines penetration ("1 in 20 Filipinos use Carousell," per Carousell's own PH marketing copy) [OTHER-PLATFORM-PRIMARY-adjacent, self-reported marketing stat: https://www.carousell.ph/].

**Mercari.** Individual Mercari US item pages are indexed and directly reachable by plain web search (confirmed empirically via `site:mercari.com item` returning live item URLs). Mercari's sold listings are hidden from the default UI but reportedly reachable by appending a `status=sold_out` URL parameter to a search, without requiring login [SECONDARY-UNVERIFIED, describes a URL-parameter trick rather than a documented API/feature: browse.sh skill-listing excerpt]. Mercari is US/Japan/UK/Taiwan-focused; it has no meaningful Philippines market presence, so it is useful only as a rough proxy for US resale pricing on categories the PH market shares (electronics, collectibles), not as PH-market comps.

**Shopee / Lazada (Philippines).** These are overwhelmingly **new-goods retail/dropship marketplaces**, not peer-to-peer secondhand marketplaces. Search results for both platforms surfaced "top selling," "bestseller," and retail category pages rather than any secondhand/used-item sold-comps mechanism, and neither platform's help center documents a "sold listings" comps feature analogous to eBay's [confirmed via this agent's WebSearch testing plus absence of any official sold-comps doc]. **Verdict: not usable as secondhand comps sources** for pricing a used item — any price signal from Shopee/Lazada reflects new-unit retail pricing, not used-market value, and should not be treated as a depreciation-adjusted comp.

**Facebook Marketplace listings themselves — indexed by web search?** This required direct empirical testing rather than trusting secondary claims (which asserted Marketplace is NOT indexed, citing robots.txt/privacy restrictions [SECONDARY-UNVERIFIED, contradicted by direct test]). **A direct `site:facebook.com/marketplace/item` search performed by this agent returned multiple live, individual Marketplace item listings** (with real titles, categories, and locations) [confirmed via this agent's own WebSearch tool]. **Finding: Facebook Marketplace item pages ARE at least partially indexed and reachable via plain web search**, contradicting the common claim that Marketplace is a closed, unindexed silo. Coverage is almost certainly partial/inconsistent (search engines don't guarantee freshness or completeness for a platform this large and geo-fragmented), and there is no way to filter these results to "sold" vs. "still active," but the categorical claim "Marketplace is invisible to search engines" is false as tested.

## 5. Depreciation by category — published evidence

**Real, primary, quantitative source: U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) depreciation rate estimates**, built on Hulten-Wykoff empirical studies of used-asset resale prices. These are official U.S. government economic-accounting depreciation rates (geometric annual decline), not marketing claims, and they are the single strongest "real data" source found in this research [RESEARCH: https://apps.bea.gov/national/pdf/BEA_depreciation_rates.pdf]. Selected rates for "Durable goods owned by consumers" (annual geometric depreciation rate = fraction of remaining value lost per year; service life in years is BEA's assumed average asset life):

| Category | Annual depreciation rate | Assumed service life |
|---|---|---|
| Furniture, including mattresses and bedsprings | 11.79% | 14 years |
| Household appliances | 15.00% | 11 years |
| Video and audio products, incl. musical instruments (proxy for consumer electronics/AV gear) | 18.33% | 9 years |
| Jewelry and watches | 27.50% | 6 years |
| Sports equipment and photographic equipment | 16.50% | 10 years |
| Glassware, tableware, household utensils | 16.50% | 10 years |
| Books and maps | 16.50% | 10 years |
| Luggage | 11.79% | 14 years |

Compounding these geometric rates: after 3 years, furniture retains roughly 69% of value, appliances ~61%, video/audio (electronics proxy) ~55%, jewelry ~36% — using BEA's own annual rates applied geometrically. Note: BEA does not publish a distinct line item for "smartphones" or "laptops" specifically; the closest categories are "Personal computers and peripheral equipment" (rate not given directly in this table — BEA cross-references a separate hedonic used-price methodology) and "video and audio products" as the general consumer-electronics proxy [RESEARCH, same source]. There's also no BEA line for "tools" in the *consumer*-durables section (there is a *government*-asset line for "power tools, lawn and garden equipment" at 16.5%/10-year life, which is a proxy only, not a consumer-market figure) [RESEARCH, same source].

Beyond BEA, no other genuinely primary academic depreciation-curve dataset for secondhand consumer goods (by category, with real transaction data) was found. What follows is industry/market-report data, useful directionally but SECONDARY:

- **Electronics:** industry commentary states smartphones/laptops/tablets retain roughly 50-70% of value after 1-3 years due to rapid model turnover [SECONDARY-UNVERIFIED, no primary dataset cited].
- **Furniture:** "good quality" secondhand furniture reportedly sells at 40-60% of original retail price, framed as holding value better than electronics [SECONDARY-UNVERIFIED].
- **Clothing:** designer/branded clothing in good condition reportedly resells at 30-50% of original price; ThredUp's own 2026 Resale Report (an industry report, not academic, but produced by a resale company with real platform transaction data) states the global secondhand apparel market grew 13% YoY in 2025 to ~$257B and that 60% of surveyed consumers now factor resale value into new-purchase decisions (up from 47% the prior year) — this is a market-trend statistic, not a per-item depreciation curve [OTHER (industry primary, ThredUp's own report, not independently audited): search-excerpt of ThredUp/WWD coverage of the 2026 Resale Report].
- **Tools:** brand-specific secondhand power-tool retention is reported at roughly 50-80% depending on brand (Milwaukee M18 FUEL 55-75%, DeWalt 50-70%, Makita 50-65%, Festool 60-80%), with commentary that tools depreciate slower than most consumer categories; pawn shops reportedly pay only 20-40% of open-market value [SECONDARY-UNVERIFIED, no dataset/methodology disclosed].
- **Baby gear:** industry data from a specialized resale company (GoodBuy Gear) states premium baby gear (strollers, car seats) commonly resells at 40-70% of retail, with a cited market-report figure that "32% of the total value of baby gear now comes from secondhand and open-box sources" [OTHER (industry primary, single resale-company's own report, not independently verified): https://goodbuygear.com/pages/resale-report-2025].

**Honest gap:** no peer-reviewed academic paper with real cross-category depreciation curves for consumer secondhand goods (beyond BEA's asset-accounting rates, which are built for national-accounts purposes, not consumer resale-pricing purposes) was located. One academic paper title surfaced — "A study on perceptual depreciation and product rarity for online exchange willingness of second-hand goods" (ScienceDirect) — but its full text was inaccessible (403 to automated fetch) and only the abstract-level topic, not quantitative findings, could be confirmed [RESEARCH — exists, content not verified: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959652619331853].

## 6. Markdown / price-drop timing evidence

**No platform (Meta, eBay, Carousell, Mercari) publishes its own data on optimal repricing timing/magnitude for secondhand listings.** The closest primary technical documentation is Mercari's own engineering blog describing the mechanics (not the tuned parameters or effectiveness data) of its Smart Pricing system:

- Smart Pricing uses a neural-network-based model to suggest an initial listing price and floor price from item attributes (image, title, description, category, brand, condition) [OTHER-PLATFORM-PRIMARY: https://engineering.mercari.com/en/blog/entry/pricing-guidance-system/].
- Post-listing, a sigmoid-shaped price-drop function considers the item's price position relative to a comparable-sales range, a maximum-drop percentage, the seller-set floor, demand signals (views/likes/impressions), and listing age; it can also *raise* price if demand increases [OTHER-PLATFORM-PRIMARY, same source].
- Each automatic price update triggers a visibility/search-ranking boost, and buyers who "liked" the item are notified of drops [OTHER-PLATFORM-PRIMARY for the mechanic description; the "boost" and "notify likers" claims come from secondary summaries layered on top of the engineering post, not verified word-for-word in the primary post itself — treat the boost/notification specifics as SECONDARY].
- **The engineering post contains no published statistics** on how much a price drop improves sale probability or reduces time-to-sale — it's a conceptual/methodological writeup, not an effectiveness study [OTHER-PLATFORM-PRIMARY, confirmed by direct fetch].

No equivalent Facebook, Carousell, or eBay documentation of automated/recommended markdown timing exists in what I could find.

**Adjacent, better-evidenced domain: real estate days-on-market vs. price-cut research**, which is the most rigorous "time-to-sale vs. price" literature available, though it is about houses, not secondhand consumer goods, so treat as directional only:
- An academic real estate paper, "Time on the Market and Selling Price," addresses the TOM/price relationship directly, but its full text was inaccessible to automated fetch (403); I can confirm the paper exists and is on-topic but not its exact findings [RESEARCH — exists, content not independently re-verified: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23523826_Time_on_the_Market_and_Selling_Price].
- Industry (non-academic) real estate sources report: price reductions tend to increase ~2-2.5% for every additional 30 days a property stays unsold; homes selling within 30 days are typically discounted only ~1% off list (achieving ~99% of asking); after a first price cut, time to go under contract drops to a median of ~12 days versus a median 23-day hold at the original price before that cut; multiple successive price cuts correlate with longer overall time-to-sale and buyer perception of "staleness" [SECONDARY-UNVERIFIED, real-estate-industry sourcing, not an academic dataset, and not about consumer goods].

**Verdict: no platform-published, secondhand-goods-specific data exists on optimal repricing timing/magnitude.** Anything claiming "reprice after X days by Y%" for Marketplace/Carousell/eBay used-goods selling is, at best, extrapolated from real-estate literature or vendor marketing, not measured secondhand-marketplace data.

## 7. Negotiation: asking-price vs. final-price gap, lowball magnitude, and OBO effects

This is the best-evidenced section of the research, thanks to a large peer-reviewed dataset.

**26 million eBay Best Offer negotiations (Backus, Blake, Larsen, Tadelis and related follow-on work) — the strongest primary research found in this entire report:**
- Sample: 26,454,176 unique negotiations across 34 product categories, 18,751,993 unique products, on eBay's "Best Offer" feature [RESEARCH: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10410759/, also published in PNAS: https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2218582120].
- For completed negotiations, final prices land closer to the buyer's opening offer than to the seller's list price: across all completed deals, **buyers conceded an average of only 13.56% from their opening offer, while sellers conceded 86.44% from their list price** [RESEARCH, same source]. Where sellers made a counteroffer (about 21% of negotiations), the split moderates to buyers conceding 36.67% vs. sellers 63.33% [RESEARCH, same source].
- The anchoring effect of the buyer's first offer on final price is essentially linear (R² = .997) — every percentage-point reduction in the opening offer proportionally reduces the final price [RESEARCH, same source].
- Impasse (deal failure) risk is non-linear by offer aggressiveness: offers near ~90% of list price sit in a low-impasse "safety zone"; impasse risk climbs sharply as offers drop from ~90% to ~20% of list; below ~20% of list, impasse risk saturates near 95% regardless of how much lower the offer goes [RESEARCH, same source].
- The study's own optimization suggests an "ideal" buyer first offer sits around 80% of list price when balancing price and impasse risk, though the ideal ranges from 33% to 95% of list depending on product category [RESEARCH, same source].
- A companion paper on counteroffer timing/magnitude (~26M negotiations, Group Decision and Negotiation, 2025) finds that **more ambitious counteroffers get buyers a better price but raise impasse risk, and later counteroffers (vs. early ones) get buyers a better price and lower impasse risk simultaneously** — i.e., waiting to counter is close to a free win for the buyer in these asynchronous, text-based negotiations [RESEARCH: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10726-025-09932-1].

Caveat: this is eBay's *own-platform* Best Offer feature (structured, single-item, asynchronous, largely US-market), not Facebook Marketplace/Carousell chat-based haggling. The mechanics (anchoring, non-linear impasse) are very likely to generalize qualitatively to Messenger/Carousell-chat negotiation, but the exact percentages (13.56% buyer concession, 80% "ideal" first offer) are eBay-Best-Offer-specific numbers, not directly verified for Facebook Marketplace or Carousell.

**General negotiation-anchoring literature** (not marketplace-specific) confirms the same qualitative pattern outside eBay: a meta-analysis across roughly 16 negotiation studies finds a correlation near 0.5 between the first offer and the final negotiated outcome, consistent with a real anchoring effect [RESEARCH: https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/dealmaking-daily/negotiation-research-can-use-effective-first-offer-strive-precision-nb, and meta-analytic synthesis at https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0749597825000603]. Separately, precise (non-round) first offers/list prices have been shown experimentally to anchor higher than round numbers — e.g., a $255,500 list price outperformed $255,000 or $256,000 list prices in attracting higher offers [RESEARCH: same Harvard PON summary, referencing published negotiation research].

**"OBO"/"negotiable" framing effect.** No peer-reviewed study specifically quantifying OBO's effect on final sale price on a peer-to-peer marketplace (Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace, Carousell) was found. What exists is: (a) an academic real-estate paper, "The effect of listing price strategy on real estate negotiations: An experimental study" (ScienceDirect) — on-topic but full text inaccessible to automated fetch (403), so its findings could not be independently confirmed [RESEARCH — exists, content not verified: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167487015001361]; and (b) secondary/blog claims that OBO listings typically settle 8-18% below the stated asking price, with the mechanism framed as "the seller sets an anchor while signaling flexibility, widening the buyer pool" [SECONDARY-UNVERIFIED, no cited dataset or methodology: aggregated from bardeen.ai / oreateai.com style guides]. **No published data found** quantifying whether marking a Facebook Marketplace/Carousell listing "negotiable" changes the final-price outcome versus an otherwise-identical fixed-price listing.

**Typical lowball magnitude.** No platform-published statistic on "typical" buyer lowball size (e.g., "buyers open at 40% of asking") was found for Facebook Marketplace or Carousell specifically. The eBay Best-Offer data above is the closest real evidence, and it describes opening offers concentrated in a "safety zone" near 90% of list price for offers that eventually succeed, with a long tail of very low offers (below ~20% of list) that almost always fail — but this is eBay Best Offer, not Marketplace/Carousell chat behavior, and should not be presented as a Marketplace-specific number.

## Confidence & Gaps

**High confidence (primary-sourced, directly verified):**
- BEA/Hulten-Wykoff annual depreciation rates by durable-goods category (real government economic data).
- The 26-million-negotiation eBay Best Offer research (anchoring linearity, concession asymmetry, non-linear impasse risk, counteroffer timing effects) — large-sample, peer-reviewed/preprint academic work.
- Meta's own AI price-suggestion feature and Seller-app "Performance insights" panel (officially announced by Meta newsroom).
- eBay's July 2026 sold/completed-listings login-wall change (directly consistent with this agent's own inability to reach sold data without auth).
- Carousell's structured "Make Offer" mechanic (official Carousell Help Centre articles).
- Empirical indexability testing performed directly by this agent: Facebook Marketplace, Mercari, and eBay individual item pages ARE reachable via plain web search; Carousell category/search pages are reachable; Shopee/Lazada return retail-catalog pages, not secondhand comps.

**Low confidence / unresolved contradictions:**
- Whether Facebook actually notifies buyers who saved a listing when the price drops — secondary sources directly contradict each other, and no Meta documentation was found either way.
- Whether Facebook's "Make an Offer" button is live in the Philippines — the Help Center page itself signaled "not available in your location" when accessed from a PH-associated session, suggesting it may not be, but this wasn't confirmed with an in-app test.
- Exact parameters of Facebook's "Renew" listing feature (7-day threshold, 5-use cap) — sourced only from reseller blogs, not Meta documentation.
- Current operational status of Mercari's "Smart Offers" feature (one source claims it was discontinued).

**No published data found (explicitly, rather than guessed):**
- No platform-published data on optimal markdown timing/magnitude for secondhand-goods listings specifically (Mercari's Smart Pricing methodology is documented, but its own engineering post publishes no effectiveness statistics).
- No peer-reviewed, quantified study on the effect of "OBO"/"negotiable" labeling on final sale price for peer-to-peer marketplace listings.
- No platform-published or academic depreciation-curve dataset specific to "baby gear," "tools," or "clothing" with disclosed real transaction methodology — only single-company industry reports (GoodBuy Gear, ThredUp) and undocumented blog claims (power-tool brand retention percentages).
- No data at all — official or unofficial — on Philippine-market-specific depreciation curves, negotiation gaps, or lowball magnitudes; every quantitative negotiation/depreciation figure in this report is US-market-derived and would need local validation before being applied to Philippine secondhand pricing.
- Full text of two on-topic academic papers (ScienceDirect real-estate-listing-strategy study; ScienceDirect perceptual-depreciation-and-rarity study) could not be retrieved past the paywall/403 — their existence and topic are confirmed, their specific findings are not.
