# Philippines Market Pack — Research

Scope: an **individual (non-business) seller** disposing of used personal items on **Facebook Marketplace** in the Philippines, as of 2026. Focus areas: payments/payment verification, escrow-like options, cash-on-delivery via courier, logistics providers, in-person meetup norms, tax/legal exposure, chat-culture conventions, and price-comp sources.

## 1. Payments

### 1.1 Dominant payment methods for P2P secondhand sales
The two dominant rails for Marketplace-style P2P sales in PH are (a) **cash on meetup**, and (b) **e-wallet transfer**, principally **GCash**, with **Maya** second, and **bank transfer via InstaPay/PESONet** used less often for small consumer-to-consumer amounts. [OBSERVED-CONVENTION] This ordering is not independently published by any single official source as a ranked stat; it is inferred from the fact that GCash and Maya are the two BSP-licensed e-money issuers marketed for peer-to-peer "Send Money," and from courier/marketplace ecosystem documentation treating GCash as the default digital option alongside COD. Treat the ranking itself as observed convention, not a cited government statistic.

### 1.2 GCash — Send Money vs. Express Send

[PROVIDER-OFFICIAL] https://help.gcash.com/hc/en-us/articles/44143447540505-Express-Send-Transaction-Limit-FAQS (fetch blocked; relayed via search index — verify directly in-app before relying on the exact figure)
- **Send Money+** is an upgrade path for users who need higher Express Send limits: GCash states it raises the effective monthly wallet limit to **₱3,000,000**, with unlimited send/receive transaction counts, for a flat **₱5 fee per transaction** once upgraded, until the limit resets on the 1st of the next month. ### 1.3 How a seller can genuinely verify a GCash payment
GCash's own guidance, issued as a public warning about AI-generated fake receipts, is explicit and directly answers the "screenshot vs. real proof" question:
- **Check the "Transactions" tab inside your own GCash app** — verify the reference number, sender's name, amount, and timestamp match what the buyer claims. [PROVIDER-OFFICIAL] https://mynt.com.ph/newsroom/gcash-warns-public-on-emerging-ai-generated-fake-receipts-scams-urges-users-to-always-check-transactions-tab
- **"If a transaction doesn't appear in your app's history, it should not be considered valid, even if you receive what may appear to be a legitimate-looking screenshot."** — direct GCash guidance. [PROVIDER-OFFICIAL] https://mynt.com.ph/newsroom/gcash-warns-public-on-emerging-ai-generated-fake-receipts-scams-urges-users-to-always-check-transactions-tab
- GCash's Chief Information Security Officer (Miguel Geronilla) is quoted urging users to "always verify transactions within the GCash app itself... and avoid relying solely on screenshots sent by others." [PROVIDER-OFFICIAL] https://mynt.com.ph/newsroom/gcash-warns-public-on-emerging-ai-generated-fake-receipts-scams-urges-users-to-always-check-transactions-tab
- GCash also sends a **real-time SMS notification** to the registered mobile number for every incoming transaction — a second, independent channel a seller can cross-check besides the in-app ledger. [SECONDARY, attributed to GCash guidance] https://www.sukineighbors.com/guides/how-to-verify-gcash-payments (secondary summary; corroborated in substance by the official mynt.com.ph release above)
- **Why this matters**: GCash explicitly frames the risk as *AI-generated fake receipt images* being circulated to con sellers into releasing goods before money actually lands — i.e., screenshots are trivially forgeable and must never be trusted as standalone proof of payment. [PROVIDER-OFFICIAL] https://mynt.com.ph/newsroom/gcash-warns-public-on-emerging-ai-generated-fake-receipts-scams-urges-users-to-always-check-transactions-tab
- GCash offers "Express Send Scam Insurance," a protection product tied to Express Send transactions (official article exists at https://help.gcash.com/hc/en-us/articles/18323082557465-Express-Send-Scam-Insurance) but this page returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch during this research; its exact coverage terms could not be independently confirmed here and should be checked live in the GCash Help Center before citing specifics. [PROVIDER-OFFICIAL, unverified detail]

**Practical seller rule implied by GCash's own materials**: never release an item on the strength of a buyer-sent image alone; require the seller's own app "Transactions" tab (or the accompanying SMS) to show the credit before handoff.

### 1.5 InstaPay / PESONet (BSP-regulated interbank rails)
- **InstaPay**: BSP-mandated real-time, low-value fund transfer service between participating banks/e-money issuers; per-transaction ceiling is **₱50,000**. [GOV, secondary-corroborated] Per-transaction cap confirmed consistently across GCash's and Maya's own fee/limit pages above; a primary BSP circular PDF (https://www.bsp.gov.ph/PaymentAndSettlement/Fees.pdf) exists but returned as binary/undecodable text in this research pass and could not be quoted directly — treat the ₱50,000 figure as corroborated by two provider-official sources (GCash, Maya) rather than independently confirmed against BSP's own text in this pass.
- **PESONet**: a batch, next-day-settlement transfer service with **no BSP-mandated single per-transaction ceiling** at the scheme level (used for higher-value transfers); individual banks/e-money issuers set their own product-level caps (e.g., Maya's own PESONet-out cap cited elsewhere as up to ₱300,000/transaction, ₱1,000,000/day — see search-derived Maya summary above, not independently re-verified against a Maya page in this pass). [SECONDARY / provider-summarized]
- **Fee reform**: BSP lifted its multi-year moratorium on InstaPay/PESONet fee increases in 2026, issuing rules meant to make fees "fairer, more transparent, and reflective of actual cost" — BSP's own cost estimate cited in secondary reporting puts the InstaPay interbank switch cost at roughly **₱1.50/transaction**. Contrary to expectations of fee hikes, most major banks and e-wallets responded by **eliminating or cutting transfer fees** rather than raising them. [SECONDARY, citing BSP] https://bitpinas.com/fintech/list-ph-banks-free-instapay/ ; https://cebudailynews.inquirer.net/740735/instapay-pesonet-fee-hikes-now-allowed-but-bsp-sets-limits
- **Bank confirmation for a seller**: the equivalent discipline applies — a bank-transfer "sent" screenshot from a buyer is not proof of receipt. The seller must check their **own bank/e-wallet's transaction history or await the bank's own SMS/push notification and updated available balance** before treating a sale as paid. This is the general BSP-regulated-rail analog of GCash's official "check your own Transactions tab" rule; it was not found stated as such in a single BSP-authored consumer-facing document during this research and is offered here as a reasonable extension of the same GCash-documented principle. [OBSERVED-CONVENTION / extrapolated]

### 1.6 Escrow-like options for individual sellers
- **Carousell Pay (formerly "CarouPay")**: Carousell's in-app protected-payment feature functions as escrow — Carousell holds the buyer's payment and releases it to the seller only after the buyer confirms receipt/satisfaction, or after a confirmation window lapses without a dispute. Sellers reportedly have a window (cited as **3 days**) to ship after a CarouPay-protected sale, and Carousell mediates/can refund the buyer if a dispute isn't resolved amicably. [SECONDARY, describing a PROVIDER feature] https://www.topbubbleindex.com/blog/carousell-protection-how-it-works/ ; https://support.carousell.com/hc/en-us/articles/360049640513--Philippines-CarouBiz-Billings-Payments (this Carousell PH help-center article returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch; details above are from secondary summaries and could not be independently confirmed against Carousell's own PH-specific fee/terms page in this pass)
- **No equivalent exists natively inside Facebook Marketplace.** Facebook/Meta does not provide a Philippines-specific escrow or buyer/seller protection product for Marketplace peer-to-peer chat-and-meetup transactions; Meta's Purchase Protection generally covers only items bought through **Facebook/Instagram Checkout**, which is not how Philippine Marketplace P2P sales are typically conducted. [OBSERVED-CONVENTION — no official Meta PH page confirming/denying Marketplace-specific escrow was located in this research pass; this is inferred from the general design of Facebook Marketplace as a listings-and-chat product with off-platform payment]
- **PayMongo** and similar payment gateways (Xendit, DragonPay) are business-facing checkout infrastructure, not consumer escrow products for individual sellers; they exist to let a *registered business* accept cards/e-wallets/OTC payments, and are not something a private Marketplace seller would typically integrate for a one-off sale. [SECONDARY] https://www.wazile.com/blog/top-e-commerce-or-digital-payment-gateways-in-the-philippines/
- **Practical implication**: an individual FB Marketplace seller in PH has **no true built-in escrow**. The closest analog (Carousell Pay) requires listing/transacting on Carousell instead of Facebook. On Facebook Marketplace itself, the two de facto risk-mitigations are (a) cash-on-meetup in a safe public place, or (b) courier COD (below), which shifts custody/payment risk to the logistics provider instead of a payment platform.

## 2. Cash-on-delivery (COD) via courier for an individual sender

- **LBC Express** explicitly markets COD as accessible to non-registered/individual senders — LBC's own COD page frames the service around "stay-at-home moms, and budding entrepreneurs," and states **no additional COD fee** when booked through LBC's online booking portal (lbconline.lbcexpress.com), with box fees separately priced (~₱15–20+ depending on region) and **no return-to-sender fee**. [PROVIDER-OFFICIAL] https://www.lbcexpress.com/us/cash-on-delivery
  - Remittance of collected COD cash back to the sender is offered via **branch pickup, bank deposit, or LBC's "Instant Peso Padala"** — but LBC's own page does not publish an exact remittance timeline (e.g., "within X business days"); this was not resolved in this research pass and should be confirmed directly with LBC customer care before relying on a specific turnaround. [PROVIDER-OFFICIAL, gap noted]
  - LBC's page does not address what happens if a buyer **refuses the parcel** (return-to-sender handling, whether COD or shipping fees are forfeited) — this is a confirmed gap; consult LBC directly (customercare@lbcexpress.com) for that specific policy. [GAP]
- **J&T Express**: historically, COD was **only offered to registered e-commerce/VIP business accounts** with "special discounts, fast remittance, and a dedicated VIP dashboard." As of the reporting cited, J&T **expanded COD access to walk-in customers and door-to-door pickup clients** (i.e., not limited to platform-integrated sellers), accessible via the J&T Express app or website, where a customer can "register for a COD account." This expansion appears targeted at MSMEs, and the sourcing does not clarify whether a purely casual individual (no business registration at all) can open a COD account, nor does it publish COD-specific fees or remittance timelines for walk-in senders. [SECONDARY, reporting on a PROVIDER announcement] https://malaya.com.ph/business/marketing-board/jt-express-expands-cash-on-delivery-cod-service-to-more-customers-nationwide/ ; https://www.manilatimes.net/2024/11/11/tmt-newswire/jt-express-expands-cod-for-delivery-pick-up-service-nationwide/2001245 — **could not locate J&T's own COD terms/fee page** (jtexpress.ph) with specifics in this pass; treat individual-sender eligibility and exact fees as an open question. [GAP]
- **Flash Express**: publishes a shipping-fee calculator (flashexpress.ph/fle/check-price) and states door-to-door service nationwide, 365 days, with 24-hour tracking, but this research pass could not confirm Flash's specific **COD-for-individual-sender** terms, fees, or remittance timelines from an official page — only that a price calculator exists. [PROVIDER-OFFICIAL for the general service; COD-specific individual-sender terms are a GAP]
- **General seller risk on COD refusal** (courier-agnostic, standard courier practice in PH e-commerce as widely reported in secondary sources, not confirmed against a single official document in this pass): if a buyer refuses a COD parcel, the **item is returned to the sender**, and the **seller typically absorbs the outbound (and sometimes return) shipping cost**, since COD couriers collect the item price + shipping from the buyer only upon successful delivery — a refusal means the seller receives nothing for the item and may still owe/forfeit courier fees already paid. This is standard e-commerce logistics practice in PH but was not confirmed against an LBC/J&T/Flash-specific "what happens on refusal" official policy page in this research pass. [OBSERVED-CONVENTION / SECONDARY, unconfirmed against a single official source]

**Overall COD conclusion**: LBC is the courier with the clearest **official, individual-sender-accessible** COD path (self-service online booking, no extra COD fee). J&T's COD-for-walk-in expansion is directionally confirmed but its individual (non-MSME) eligibility, fees, and remittance timeline are not fully documented in official sources found here. Flash's individual-sender COD specifics are an open gap. **Recommendation for a seller pack**: default to LBC's documented COD path, and treat J&T/Flash individual-sender COD as "ask the specific branch/app — terms vary and are not fully published."

### 3.4 Which are actually used for Marketplace handoffs
[OBSERVED-CONVENTION] Lalamove and GrabExpress (both same-day, rider-dispatched, no drop-off required) are the couriers most naturally suited to — and anecdotally used for — direct **buyer-arranges-rider** Marketplace handoffs, especially within the same city/region, because the buyer or seller can book a single point-to-point trip without needing an account with the parcel company or a drop-off/pickup schedule. J&T and Flash (multi-day, branch-network parcel couriers) are more typically used when buyer and seller are in **different provinces/islands** and same-day pickup isn't feasible. This ordering is drawn from the products' own structural differences (on-demand vs. scheduled-network) rather than from a single cited usage survey — labeled OBSERVED-CONVENTION accordingly.

## 4. Meetup norms

- **Mall meetups** are the most commonly cited "safe" venue in PH online-selling culture — public, well-lit, camera-covered, with security guards and foot traffic. [OBSERVED-CONVENTION, corroborated by general (non-PH-specific) safety-tip sources] e.g., https://offertrackr.com/blog/places-to-meet — note this source is US-generic, not PH-specific; the mall-meetup convention itself is PH-observed common practice, not sourced from a PH government or corporate document in this pass.
- **"Meet up sa MRT/LRT station"** and **"sa may [landmark]"** (e.g., outside a 7-Eleven, a jeepney terminal, a barangay hall) are common shorthand in PH Marketplace/Viber/FB-group chat for proposing a meeting point — these are colloquial conventions, not documented in any official source located in this research. [OBSERVED-CONVENTION]
- **PNP Anti-Cybercrime Group (PNP-ACG)** is the relevant government unit; it has run public-awareness partnerships (e.g., with **SM Supermalls**, via a Memorandum of Agreement giving PNP free airtime on SM Cinemas/malls for cybercrime-prevention messaging) and has publicly flagged **online-selling scams as among the most common cybercrime complaint categories** (one cited figure: 1,630 cases in a recent reporting period). [SECONDARY, reporting on PNP-ACG activity] https://www.manilatimes.net/2025/09/06/supplements/sm-supermalls-pnp-anti-cybercrime-group-join-forces-to-promote-public-awareness-on-cybersecurity/2180023 ; https://palawan-news.com/pnp-issues-safety-tips-as-cybercrime-complaints-continue-to-grow/
- **No specific, citable PNP circular or official webpage** establishing formally designated "safe transaction zones" (analogous to some US police departments' marked "Safe Exchange Zone" parking spots) was located for the Philippines in this research pass. What exists instead: (a) PNP-ACG general public-safety-awareness messaging (mall partnerships, social media advisories) urging caution with online sellers/buyers, and (b) the informal, widely-observed practice of using a **police station's or barangay hall's parking area/lobby** as a self-selected safe meetup point — this practice is culturally common but is **not** documented as an official, PNP-sanctioned program in the sources found here. [GAP — flagged explicitly] Treat "meet at the police station" as an OBSERVED-CONVENTION good practice, not a formal PNP program, absent a located PNP circular establishing one.
- Recommendation defensible from what is documented: prefer a **mall interior, in front of a manned/busy store**, during **daytime**, and if using a police-adjacent location, treat it as a self-arranged safety choice (officers will not mediate the transaction), consistent with how equivalent foreign police-run programs describe their own role ("staff will not get involved in transactions"). [SECONDARY, extrapolated principle — the specific "will not get involved" language sourced here is from a non-PH police department's program description, used only to state the general, cross-jurisdiction principle that police presence at a meetup is passive/deterrent, not transactional mediation]

## 5. Legal & tax obligations for an individual casual reseller

> **Not legal or tax advice.** The following is a plain-language summary of publicly available primary-source text and secondary commentary as of 2026. Tax and consumer-protection classification questions (e.g., "am I 'engaged in business'?") are fact-specific and enforcement-discretion-dependent. **Verify your specific situation with a BIR-accredited tax practitioner or lawyer before relying on any interpretation below**, especially if selling frequently, in volume, or for profit rather than merely disposing of personal used items.

### 5.1 Republic Act 11967 — Internet Transactions Act (ITA) of 2023
- RA 11967 creates an Electronic Commerce Bureau under DTI and regulates **online merchants, e-retailers, and e-marketplaces** engaged in internet transactions. [GOV] https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2023/ra_11967_2023.html
- **Scope**: the Act applies to **business-to-business (B2B) and business-to-consumer (B2C)** internet transactions within DTI's mandate. [GOV] https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2023/ra_11967_2023.html
- **Consumer-to-consumer (C2C) transactions are excluded from the Act's coverage.** The IRR/commentary defines C2C as "transactions between end-users done for personal, family, or household purposes and not done in [the] ordinary course of business." [GOV/IRR, corroborated by secondary summary] https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2023/ra_11967_2023.html ; commentary confirming the C2C carve-out: search-index summary of IRR matrix materials (Joint Administrative Order No. 24-03, s. 2024) — the primary IRR PDF itself was not directly re-fetched in this pass, so this exclusion should be **spot-checked against the IRR text** before being treated as fully authoritative, though it is consistently and independently reported across multiple secondary legal-commentary sources.
- **Practical read for a casual individual FB Marketplace seller**: a private person selling their own used personal items to another private individual is a textbook C2C transaction — done for personal/household purposes, not in the ordinary course of business — and therefore **appears to fall outside RA 11967's direct compliance obligations** (no requirement to register as an "online merchant," display DTI business info, etc.) **so long as the activity genuinely stays casual/personal and does not shade into "ordinary course of business."** [GOV-based inference, not an explicit government FAQ answer — flagged as the single most important ambiguity in this whole pack] The law does **not** define a bright-line frequency/volume/revenue threshold distinguishing "casual personal disposal" from "ordinary course of business" for an individual using Facebook Marketplace specifically; that line is genuinely ambiguous and would be assessed case-by-case (e.g., by DTI or, for tax purposes, BIR) based on facts like transaction frequency, whether items are bought-to-resell vs. personal-use disposal, and income pattern. **This is a real, unresolved gray area — flagged, not resolved.**

### 5.2 BIR — withholding tax on e-marketplace/DFSP remittances (RR 16-2023) and general registration duty
- **RR No. 16-2023** requires **e-marketplace operators** (e.g., Shopee, Lazada) and **digital financial services providers (DFSPs)** — defined as entities holding/safekeeping funds on behalf of a buyer/consumer in a mobile payment service context — to withhold **1% income tax on one-half of gross remittances** paid to sellers/merchants for goods/services sold **through their platform/facility**. Effective **January 11, 2024** (15 days after Dec. 27, 2023 publication); implementation of the actual withholding start was later reported as beginning **July 15, 2024**. [GOV, corroborated by secondary reporting] https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/companies/913492/bir-withholding-tax-on-online-sellers-started-july-15/story/ ; RR text: https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%2016-2023.pdf (not directly re-fetched/quoted in this pass; relied on secondary legal-commentary summaries which are broadly consistent across multiple firms)
- **₱500,000 threshold**: sellers/merchants whose **cumulative gross remittances from all e-marketplace operators and DFSPs combined** do not exceed **₱500,000** in a taxable year are **exempt** from this specific withholding mechanism. [SECONDARY, summarizing RR 16-2023] https://www.tripleiconsulting.com/what-philippine-rr-16-2023-withholding-tax-online-sellers/
- **Does this apply to a peer-to-peer Facebook Marketplace sale?** This is the second key ambiguity to flag clearly:
  - RR 16-2023's withholding mechanism is triggered by remittances **made by an e-marketplace operator or DFSP to a seller for sales made through that platform/facility**. A private individual selling on Facebook Marketplace and getting paid by a buyer **directly** via a peer-to-peer GCash "Send Money"/Express Send transfer, bank transfer, or cash **is not receiving a "remittance" from an e-marketplace operator or DFSP acting as a payment collector/aggregator for platform sales** — GCash/Maya are not "remitting" the seller's marketplace earnings in that structural sense; they are simply facilitating a direct wallet-to-wallet transfer between two private users. On the plain terms of RR 16-2023 as summarized in the sources reviewed, **this withholding regime targets Shopee/Lazada/TikTok-Shop-style seller payouts, not casual GCash-to-GCash P2P transfers for a Facebook Marketplace sale.** [Reasoned inference from GOV regulation text as summarized in SECONDARY sources — **not confirmed by a BIR FAQ specifically addressing Facebook Marketplace or GCash P2P transfers**, and should be verified with a tax professional, particularly if the individual is using Facebook Shop's checkout/payment features rather than off-platform chat-and-transfer.]
  - Separately from the RR 16-2023 withholding mechanism, **general BIR registration/income-tax obligations exist independent of any platform** — under longstanding BIR rules (not specific to online selling), **any person "doing business"** (buying/reselling for profit on a regular basis) is generally expected to register with BIR, issue receipts, and declare income, **regardless of the sales channel** (online or offline). The genuinely difficult, fact-specific question — which this research **cannot answer with certainty and flags explicitly** — is where an individual's occasional decluttering/reselling of their own used personal belongings (not bought-to-resell, not regular/repeated as a livelihood) stops being "merely disposing of personal property" (generally not taxable business income) and starts being "engaged in business" in BIR's eyes (taxable, registrable). BIR has not published a bright-line frequency/peso threshold specifically for "casual personal-item resellers on Facebook Marketplace." [GOV-based general principle, GAP on the specific bright line — consult a professional for anything beyond occasional, low-value personal-item disposal.]

### 5.3 Consumer Act (RA 7394)
- RA 7394 defines **"seller"** and **"retailer"** as *persons engaged in the business of* selling consumer products directly to consumers, and **"manufacturer"**/**"distributor"** similarly by reference to commercial activity. [GOV] https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1992/ra_7394_1992.html
- On these definitions, the Act's substantive obligations (warranty rules, labeling, price-tag requirements, prohibitions on deceptive sales acts, etc.) are **aimed at commercial actors "engaged in business,"** and an isolated, casual sale of a used personal item by a private individual who is not in the business of selling **does not squarely fit the Act's "seller"/"retailer" definitions.** [GOV-based inference] This is broadly consistent with the C2C exclusion logic under RA 11967 above — both bodies of law structurally distinguish commercial sellers from private individuals disposing of personal property. **This is still an inference from definitional text, not a specific DTI ruling on Facebook Marketplace resellers**, and general consumer-protection doctrines (e.g., liability for actual fraud or misrepresentation, which can arise under the Civil Code regardless of "business" status) still apply to any seller, individual or commercial.

### 5.4 Data Privacy Act (RA 10173) relevance to a private seller
- The National Privacy Commission (NPC) has published guidance directed at **"online merchants/sellers"**, warning against unauthorized disclosure/processing and improper disposal of customers' personal data, and advising sellers to (a) collect only data necessary to the transaction, (b) be transparent about use of that data, and (c) apply reasonable security measures — the same standard NPC applies to physical stores. [GOV] https://privacy.gov.ph/online-sellers-told-to-protect-customers-personal-data-from-unauthorized-disclosure-and-improper-disposal/
- This NPC guidance is phrased broadly ("online merchants/sellers") rather than explicitly carved to exclude casual individuals; a private Marketplace seller who collects a buyer's name, address, and phone number for delivery should, as a matter of basic good practice consistent with NPC's stated principles, avoid keeping that data longer than needed and not share/sell it onward. Whether the Data Privacy Act's **registration/compliance officer requirements** (which formally apply to entities processing personal data at a certain scale) reach a single individual doing occasional Marketplace sales is not addressed by name in the NPC page found here and is a further ambiguity; for a casual seller handling a handful of buyers' delivery details, the realistic takeaway is **"handle buyer contact info carefully and don't misuse it,"** not "you must formally register as a data controller." [GOV-based general guidance; specific individual-scale applicability is a GAP]

## 6. Language & culture in PH Marketplace chat

All items in this section are **[OBSERVED-CONVENTION]** unless otherwise noted — they describe widely-used colloquial practice in Philippine online buy-and-sell culture rather than content from a government or corporate source.

- **"Still available po?"** — the near-universal opener a buyer sends on a listing; "po" is the Filipino honorific/politeness particle appended to show respect, used regardless of the addressee's age or status in a commercial-sounding exchange. "Opo" is its affirmative-reply counterpart ("Yes po" / polite "yes").
- **Haggling ("tawad")**: asking for a discount is a deeply normalized, expected part of PH buy-and-sell culture, both offline and now in Marketplace chat. Common phrasing: *"Pwede pa po ba tawad?"* ("Can I still get a discount?"), or simply **"LP?"** ("last price?") as shorthand for "what's your absolute lowest price."
- **"RFS"** = "Reason For Selling" — buyers commonly ask this on used-item listings, in part because the answer sometimes reveals a hidden defect (e.g., a phone sold because of battery issues). [SECONDARY] https://www.paymongo.com/blog/online-selling-terms-philippines
- **"NP"** in PH selling shorthand is documented (per the PayMongo glossary below) as **"Negotiable Price,"** not "no problem" — flagging this because "NP = no problem" is a common general-internet abbreviation but is **not** what the PH-specific online-selling glossary source uses it for. [SECONDARY] https://www.paymongo.com/blog/online-selling-terms-philippines
- **"Reserved"** — signals an item is being held for a specific interested buyer pending payment/pickup, taking it off active availability without formally marking it sold.
- **"Sana all"** — a general PH internet-culture phrase ("hopefully [that applies to] all [of us]," i.e., "must be nice," often used enviously/aspirationally) that appears in Marketplace comment sections reacting to a great deal or a seller's generosity — not selling-specific slang, but common in listing comment threads.
- **Selected abbreviation glossary** (compiled from a Philippine-specific online-selling terms guide): [SECONDARY] https://www.paymongo.com/blog/online-selling-terms-philippines
  - Browsing/status: **LF**/**ISO** (looking for / in search of), **FS** (for sale), **FT** (for trade), **OH** (on hand/ready to ship), **HTF** (hard to find), **OOS** (out of stock), **UP** (bump post to top).
  - Condition: **BNS/BNIB/BNWT** (brand-new sealed / in box / with tags), **NBU** (never been used), **DOP** (date of purchase), **RFS** (reason for selling), **WW** (when worn — for pre-loved clothing).
  - Price/negotiation: **LP** (lowest/last price), **SRP** (suggested retail price), **DP** (down payment), **NP** (negotiable price), **FIRM** (non-negotiable), **RESERVED**.
  - Payment/delivery: **MOP** (mode of payment), **MOD** (mode of delivery), **COD**, **SF** (shipping fee), **MU** (meet up), **OTW** (on the way), **FULL** (full payment sent).
  - Live-selling chat: **MINE**/**FF** (fast fingers — claiming an item in a live sale), **CF** (confirm), **PAID**, **CO** (checkout), **SOLD**.
- These glossary terms should be treated as **broadly representative, not exhaustively standardized** — usage varies by platform, seller community, and region, and the single glossary source cited here (PayMongo, a payments company blog, not a linguistic or government authority) is the only documented compilation found in this research pass.
