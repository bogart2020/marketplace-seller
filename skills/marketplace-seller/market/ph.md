# Philippines

The active market pack. Currency **PHP (₱)**. Write all listings and replies in **English**.

## Ten listings a week

Meta's own listing-limits page states: **"In the Philippines, you can post up to 10 listings per week on Facebook Marketplace."** Limits vary by country; this is the PH figure.

Posting slots are the scarcest resource in the whole operation, so spend them deliberately:

- **Renew before you relist.** Deleting and re-posting spends a slot. Renewing does not. Note that Meta documents neither the renewal mechanic nor any ranking benefit for it — see the honesty note below.
- **Rank the queue by expected value**, not by what was photographed most recently. Highest expected sale price and fastest expected sale go first.
- **Fix before reposting.** An item that failed on price or photos will fail again at the cost of another slot.
- **Track the week.** When more items are ready than slots remain, say so and let the human choose the order.

## Payment

**Money exists when it appears in your own app.** The full rule and the GCash statement behind it are in `safety.md`; it is the single most important line in this pack.

**GCash** dominates peer-to-peer. Verify in the app's **Transactions** tab — reference number, sender, amount, timestamp — and cross-check the SMS sent to your registered number. GCash has publicly warned that AI-generated fake receipts are circulating.

**Maya** works the same way; apply identical discipline to its activity log. *(GCash publishes the don't-trust-screenshots rule explicitly; Maya's equivalent statement was not located.)*

**Bank and InstaPay** transfers cap at **₱50,000 per transaction** across providers. Above that, PESONet settles next-day at bank-set limits. Wait for your own bank's notification and balance, never the sender's screenshot.

**Cash** stays the default for meetups. Count before handing over.

**There is no escrow on Facebook Marketplace.** Meta's Purchase Protection covers onsite checkout and shipped orders, US-only and capped — not a PH meetup. Carousell Pay is the nearest local equivalent and requires selling on Carousell instead.

## Handoff

**Same city — the buyer books a rider.** Lalamove motorcycle starts at **₱49** plus ₱6/km for the first 5 km; sedan from ₱100, small van from ₱200. GrabExpress bike covers up to 20 kg within a **30 km** cap, with automatic insurance to **₱20,000**. Both price dynamically, so quote from the app, not from these figures.

Have the **buyer book and pay for the rider**, and confirm payment for the item before the rider collects. A rider is not an escrow agent — handing goods to a rider before the money lands is the proxy-pickup pattern in `safety.md`.

**Different province — parcel courier.** J&T and Flash run multi-day nationwide door-to-door networks priced by weight; use their own calculators at the time of shipping.

**COD.** LBC has the clearest officially documented path for an individual sender: self-service booking at lbconline.lbcexpress.com, **no extra COD fee**, no return-to-sender fee, box fees around ₱15–20. Collected cash comes back by branch pickup, bank deposit, or Instant Peso Padala — **LBC publishes no remittance timeline**, so confirm it with them before depending on it. J&T has extended COD beyond business accounts, but individual eligibility, fees, and timelines are not published. Flash's individual-sender COD terms are undocumented.

**COD's real risk is refusal.** If the buyer declines the parcel, the item comes back and the seller generally absorbs the shipping — you get neither the item's price nor your freight. Recommend COD only when the buyer has given a reason to trust the address, and prefer it over accepting a screenshot as proof of payment. *(Refusal handling is standard PH courier practice, not confirmed against any courier's published policy.)*

## Meetups

Malls in daylight, in front of a busy staffed store, are the working convention — public, lit, covered by cameras and guards. MRT and LRT stations and named landmarks are the usual shorthand for proposing a spot.

The PNP Anti-Cybercrime Group runs public-awareness work and reports online-selling scams among its most common complaint categories, but **no formal PNP "safe transaction zone" programme was found**. Meeting at a police station or barangay hall is a sensible self-arranged choice, not an official service — officers will not mediate the transaction. Say it that way rather than implying a programme exists.

## Comps

**Carousell PH is the primary comps source** — a genuine peer-to-peer resale market, indexed and readable without login. Asking prices only; no sold-price filter exists.

**Facebook Marketplace itself** is worth searching directly; item pages do surface in web search, though coverage is uneven. The human's own in-app search is better than any fetch, because it is geo-personalised to their city.

**Shopee and Lazada** give the retail anchor — what the item costs new. They are not secondhand comps. Deep product pages may prompt login, so treat a failed fetch as normal and ask the human to look.

## Tax and legal

**Not legal or tax advice. A BIR-accredited practitioner or a lawyer should confirm anything below for a specific situation.**

The Internet Transactions Act (RA 11967) governs B2B and B2C internet transactions and **excludes consumer-to-consumer** ones — defined as transactions between end-users for personal, family, or household purposes, outside the ordinary course of business. A private person selling their own used belongings sits squarely in that exclusion. The Consumer Act's obligations likewise attach to those "engaged in the business of" selling.

**This matters for a buy-to-resell operation.** The C2C exclusion covers disposing of your own property. Buying stock in order to resell it, repeatedly and for profit, is what "ordinary course of business" describes — and BIR's general registration and income-declaration duties attach to doing business regardless of channel, online or off. **Neither DTI nor BIR publishes a bright-line frequency or peso threshold** separating casual disposal from doing business; it is assessed case by case on facts like whether items were bought to resell, how often, and the income pattern. This is a genuine unresolved grey area — flag it plainly rather than reassuring anyone.

BIR's **RR 16-2023** withholding (1% on half of gross remittances, with a ₱500,000 annual threshold) is triggered by e-marketplace operators and payment providers remitting seller payouts — the Shopee and Lazada model. A direct GCash transfer between two private people for a Marketplace sale is structurally not such a remittance. *(This is a reading of the regulation, not a BIR ruling on Facebook Marketplace or GCash P2P transfers.)*

On buyer data: collect only what the handoff needs, keep it no longer than necessary, and never pass it on. The National Privacy Commission's guidance to online sellers says as much, and it is the right default whatever the registration thresholds turn out to mean for an individual.

## Honesty notes

Two things sellers will ask about that Meta does not document, and the skill should not pretend otherwise:

- **The renew mechanic.** No published eligibility window, no cap, no stated visibility effect. The "renew every 7 days, five times maximum" advice everywhere online is folklore, confirmed absent from Meta's pages.
- **Responsiveness and badges.** Marketplace lists a Very Responsive badge with no published threshold. The 90%-in-15-minutes figure describes Facebook *Pages*, a different surface. The only distribution factor Meta states explicitly is shipping performance.
