# Buyer chat

Drafts replies. Every message gets screened before it gets answered — `safety.md` holds the scam tells, and a scam that reaches the negotiation stage has already cost you.

Messages arrive in Messenger and in the Marketplace inbox; they are the same conversation. Keep it there. A buyer moving you to SMS, WhatsApp, or Viber early is removing the record.

## Triage

Facebook gives buyers a prefilled **"Is this still available?"** button. Most openers are a tap, not a decision — the buyer may not have read the listing or the price. Volume here says nothing about demand.

Answer it in one short message that confirms availability and asks something only a real buyer answers:

> Yes, available. When would you like to pick it up?

That single question sorts the inbox. Buyers who name a time are real; buyers who vanish were never going to arrive. Spend effort in proportion to what comes back.

**Answer questions the listing already covers, without pointing that out.** "Is this original?" and "what's the issue?" are trust checks, not reading failures. Repeat the answer plainly and add the photo number.

## Negotiating

The evidence and the numbers live in `pricing.md`. Three rules drive the drafts:

**Always counter.** Never a flat yes or a flat no. In 26 million eBay negotiations, sellers who countered conceded 63% of the gap instead of 86%. A counter is worth real money on almost every deal.

**Never counter instantly.** Acknowledge quickly, concede slowly — later counteroffers did better *and* failed less often. Reply promptly; move on price on your own schedule.

**Hold the floor.** The floor from `item.md` is never spoken, never hinted at, and never appears in a draft. When an offer sits below it, counter above it or decline warmly.

Answer "LP?" or "last price?" with a number, not a question back. It is a normal, expected opener in the Philippines and refusing to engage reads as unfriendly. Give a figure above your floor and tie it to a condition — collection today, or meeting near you.

> I can do ₱3,450 if you can collect today. I'll meet you at Trinoma.

When an offer lands below roughly a fifth of the ask, deals essentially never close from there. Reply once, politely, near the ask, and stop spending attention on it.

## Holding an item

Hold against a deposit or a named time. Nothing else.

> I can hold it until 6pm today — after that it goes back up.

"Reserved" without either is how an item sits unavailable for a week and sells to nobody. Set `status: pending` only when a deposit has landed or a specific handoff time is agreed; when the buyer ghosts, return it to `listed` and log the no-show.

## Responsiveness

Meta lists a **Very Responsive** badge among Marketplace seller badges, but publishes no threshold for it. The 90%-in-15-minutes figure in circulation is documented for Facebook *Pages*, a different surface, and the "Fast Replier" rules quoted in seller guides are unsourced. Meta's one explicit statement about distribution concerns shipping performance, not reply speed.

So reply fast because buyers with cash go to whoever answers first — not because a documented ranking benefit exists. Say it that way if asked.

## Philippine conventions

**Write every reply in English** — clear, friendly, and plain. Buyers will often write in Taglish or in the shorthand below; read it fluently and answer in English without correcting them or mirroring the slang back.

Haggling — **tawad** — is expected and carries no rudeness. Treat the first ask for a discount as the opening of a normal conversation.

| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| LP | Last or lowest price |
| NP | **Negotiable price** — not "no problem" |
| FIRM | Non-negotiable |
| RFS | Reason for selling |
| MOP / MOD | Mode of payment / mode of delivery |
| MU | Meet up |
| SF | Shipping fee |
| OTW | On the way |
| FULL | Full payment sent |
| DP | Down payment |
| OH | On hand, ready to ship |
| BNIB / BNWT / NBU | Brand new in box / with tags / never been used |

Expect **RFS** on used items — buyers ask because the answer sometimes reveals a defect. Answer it straight; evasion here reads exactly like the scam pattern Meta warns buyers about.

*(This glossary comes from a single Philippine online-selling compilation, not a standards body. Usage varies by community — follow the buyer's vocabulary rather than correcting it.)*

## Ending it

Report and block a buyer who pressures, threatens, or runs a pattern from `safety.md`. Reporting runs through the message thread: **Marketplace → Selling → Seller Dashboard → Your Listings → the listing → the thread → Options → Report the Buyer.** Meta documents the path but not what happens next, so treat reporting as protecting the next seller rather than as recourse for you.

You are never obliged to complete a sale. Withdrawing beats a handoff that feels wrong.
