# Safety

Reached when money or a handoff is involved: a buyer proposes payment, a meetup is being arranged, or a message smells wrong. Two jobs — confirm the money is real, and make the handoff safe.

## You are the only protection

Meta's Purchase and Seller Protection covers items bought through onsite checkout and shipped: US-only, capped at $500, with a shipping window. A local meetup paid in cash or e-wallet sits entirely outside it.

Facebook Marketplace has no escrow. In the Philippines the nearest equivalent, Carousell Pay, requires selling on Carousell instead.

So there is no recourse layer under a Marketplace handoff. Every rule below stands alone, which is why they are worth following exactly rather than approximately.

State this plainly the first time a human proposes releasing an item on a promise. Say it once, clearly, then help.

## The payment rule

**Money is received when it appears in your own account. Nothing else counts.**

Open your own app and confirm the credit in your transaction history — reference number, sender name, amount, and timestamp all matching what the buyer claims. GCash states this directly, in its own warning about AI-generated fake receipts:

> "If a transaction doesn't appear in your app's history, it should not be considered valid, even if you receive what may appear to be a legitimate-looking screenshot."

GCash also sends an SMS to the registered number for every incoming transaction — a second independent channel to cross-check.

Apply the identical discipline to Maya, to bank transfers, and to InstaPay: check your own ledger or your own bank's notification. *(GCash publishes this rule explicitly; the extension to Maya and banks is the same principle, not a quote from those providers.)*

A buyer-sent image is not evidence. Screenshots are trivially forged, and generated receipts now clear the bar of looking real. When a human says "he already sent proof," ask one question: **does it show in your app?**

## Scam tells

Each line is the observable signal. Match on the pattern, not on how the buyer sounds — the effective ones are polite and unhurried.

| Pattern | Tell |
|---|---|
| Verification-code / Google Voice | Asks you to send them a code you just received, or to "verify you're real" with one. A code sent to your number is never for someone else. |
| Fake payment screenshot | Payment "proof" arrives as an image and the buyer presses for release. Nothing in your own app. |
| Overpayment and refund | Pays more than the price, then asks for the difference back. The original payment reverses later. |
| Zelle/PayPal "business upgrade" | Claims your account must be upgraded to receive their payment, and that they'll cover the fee. Real transfers never require the recipient to pay first. |
| Phishing confirmation link | Payment confirmation arrives as a link to log in or "release" funds. Real credits need no action from you. |
| Buyer's own courier or label | Insists on their courier or sends you a prepaid label. Moves custody to someone they control. |
| Proxy pickup | An agent, driver, or relative collects while the buyer pays "after." Two strangers, one of whom is never accountable. |
| QR code to "receive" | Sends a QR for you to scan to get paid. Scanning a payment QR sends money — it does not receive it. |
| COD manipulation | Books COD, then refuses the parcel or supplies a bad address. You absorb shipping both ways. |

Two signals cut across all of them: **pressure to release before the money lands**, and **any request to move off Messenger** early. Either one alone justifies slowing down.

When a message matches, say which pattern and which tell fired. A human who can see the mechanism can judge the next message without asking.

## The handoff

**Public, daylight, populated.** Malls, transit stations, and busy commercial areas are the working norm in the Philippines; some police stations elsewhere run designated exchange zones. *(No Meta page recommending police-station meetups was found, and no formal PNP safe-zone program was located — these are conventions, not published programs.)*

**Bring someone, or tell someone.** Share the meetup place and time with a person who is not going.

**Confirm the money before the item leaves your hands.** In person this is the same rule as above: your app, not their screen. Cash gets counted before the handover, and checked for authenticity on high-value sales.

**Home addresses stay private until you choose otherwise.** Where handing over at home is unavoidable — heavy furniture, appliances — arrange daylight, and have another adult present.

**Leaving is always available.** A buyer who arrives with less than the agreed amount, brings unexpected people, or pushes to change the terms on the spot has told you what the rest of the transaction will be. The item is worth less than the risk.

## Cash on delivery

COD moves payment risk to the courier instead of removing it. Before recommending it, check `market/ph.md` for which couriers actually remit COD to individual senders, on what timeline, and who absorbs the cost of a refused parcel — the answer differs by courier and is the whole basis of the decision.

## When it has already gone wrong

Report the buyer and the conversation to Facebook, and block. If money moved, contact the payment provider immediately — speed matters most in the first hours. Keep the thread: screenshots of the conversation, the listing, and any transaction references. Do not pursue the buyer directly.
