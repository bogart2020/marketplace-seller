# Commerce Policy

Reached when an item may be unlistable, or when a listing was rejected. Produce one of three verdicts: **listable**, **listable after changes**, or **not listable here**. Name the category you matched against so the human can judge the call themselves.

Meta applies these rules to any attempt to buy, sell, trade, or give away across Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Messenger — a free giveaway is still commerce content.

Source: <https://www.facebook.com/policies_center/commerce>. The page auto-localizes, so paraphrase a rule rather than quoting wording as Meta's exact text.

## Traps that catch honest sellers

These are the ones a reasonable person lists without a second thought. Check an item against this list before the full list above — it catches far more real cases.

| Item | Category matched | Why it surprises people |
|------|------------------|-------------------------|
| Vitamins, supplements, protein powder | Medical and healthcare products | "Medical" reads as prescription-only; ingestibles are covered too |
| Used blood-pressure monitor, nebulizer, thermometer | Medical and healthcare products | A used medical *device* is prohibited, not merely regulated |
| Any downloadable, account, subscription, or licence key | Subscriptions and digital products | Marketplace sells physical items |
| Offering a service, a rental, or your labour | Subscriptions and digital products / Jobs | The listing has no physical item to hand over |
| Pets, livestock, rehoming an animal | Land, animals, and animal products | Prohibited even when free and well-intentioned |
| Vapes, e-cigarettes, pods, and their accessories | Tobacco products and paraphernalia | Paraphernalia extends past tobacco itself |
| Opened perfume, lipstick, foundation | Used cosmetics | Prohibited once outside original packaging |
| Secondhand cribs, car seats, older electronics | Recalled products | Being unaware of the recall is not a defence — check the item against the manufacturer or regulator first |
| An "in search of" post, a poll, a joke listing | No item for sale | A post with no actual product is a commerce violation |

Weight-loss framing and before-and-after imagery draw enforcement even on otherwise-listable items. Describe the object, never a change it will produce in the buyer. *(Confidence: consistent with Meta's misleading-claims and personal-attributes policies; not confirmed on a Marketplace-specific page.)*

Gift cards and vouchers appear to be restricted to approved sellers, and event-ticket resale did not appear as a named clause in the fetched policy text. *(Confidence: low — treat both as risky rather than settled, and say so when advising.)*

## What gets a compliant item rejected

The item is allowed but the listing is not:

- **Personal-attribute targeting** — copy implying something about the viewer's body, health, or finances. Write about the object.
- **Stock or generic photos** instead of the actual item, or photos that do not match the description. Both read as "no item for sale".
- **Overlay text and watermarks** on photos. Keep the price in the price field, where buyers filter on it anyway. *(Confidence: widely reported by sellers; not confirmed in Meta's policy text.)*
- **Counterfeits** — named explicitly by Meta as a rejection reason.

## After a rejection

Meta shows the specific reason under **Your listings → Needs attention → See details**. Read the stated reason before appealing; it names the category, which usually decides whether an appeal is worth filing at all.

To appeal: profile → Marketplace → "See what happened" on the listing → **Request review**.

Three limits, from Meta's own help page:

- Nothing can be reviewed more than **180 days** after rejection.
- **One** review request per listing — there is no second attempt.
- Some violations are **not eligible for review**.

Because the request is single-use, decide first whether the listing genuinely fits an allowed category. A listing that matched a real prohibited category is better relisted as a compliant item than spent on an appeal.

For a restricted account, **Settings → Account Quality** shows the restriction and any available review control. *(Confidence: navigation path from secondary sources; turnaround times and appeal length limits circulating online are unconfirmed — avoid quoting them.)*

## Repeated violations

Meta enforces proportionally to severity, account history, and risk, escalating from warning through feature restrictions to disabling. Marketplace access is a feature that gets restricted well before an account is disabled, so an early Commerce Policy strike is worth taking seriously rather than testing.

Duplicate listings of one item have **no rule found in Meta's published policy**. Sellers report near-identical listings being pruned to one. Treat repeated posting as unproven risk, not as a documented violation — and say which of those it is when asked.
